## What the combined evidence says

### 1. Two synthetic onset-robustness interventions failed for different reasons

S12 used recursive frame-wide onset softening. It improved the synthetic soft-onset challenge in all three paired seeds, but the mean gains were below the preregistered floors and ordinary synthetic competence regressed substantially:
- challenge pitch-onset F1 mean delta **+0.0370**;
- challenge recall mean delta **+0.0413**;
- ordinary pitch-onset F1 mean delta **-0.0508**;
- ordinary state admission mean delta **-0.0775**;
- ordinary joint admission mean delta **-0.0646**.

The subsequent model-free review showed that S12's transform was not a clean identity-preserving view: it modified all 192 bins, altered following frames, and recursively coupled adjacent onsets.

S13 replaced that transform with the narrower positive-onset-increment compression and froze one single intervention. Its challenge result did not improve:
- challenge pitch-onset F1 mean delta **-0.0121**;
- challenge recall mean delta **-0.0233**;
- challenge F1 positive in only **1/3** seeds;
- challenge recall positive in **0/3** seeds.

S13 also breached ordinary precision/state-admission preservation limits in individual seeds.

Together, these results reject both exact interventions. They do **not** establish that onset robustness is impossible.

### 2. The current synthetic simulator is useful for controlled engineering, but is too narrow to be treated as a real-guitar proxy

The S0 corpus is a repo-owned procedural plucked-string surrogate:
- **294** clips;
- **7** families;
- 2-second clips;
- deterministic analytic harmonic/decay/transient synthesis;
- CQT features generated by the same frozen frontend used by the synthetic experiments.

This is valuable for reproducibility and unit-scale causal comparisons. However, its acoustic generator is deliberately simple. It does not establish coverage of real pickup/electronics chains, room/cabinet response, player dynamics, noise topology, fret/string interactions, compression/distortion, microphone/direct-input differences, or performer-specific articulation.

The failed real transfer plus the S12/S13 behavior is therefore more consistent with a **simulator/representation gap risk** than with a need for another local synthetic transform search. That is an inference from the pattern of failures, not a proof of one unique cause.

### 3. The current model has limited inductive structure for real-domain transfer

The frozen S11 model flattens five CQT frames into a 960-dimensional vector and applies:
- one Linear(960,128) + ReLU shared encoder;
- an MLP state head;
- a linear six-string onset head.

It does not explicitly model local time-frequency patterns, harmonic translation, string-source separation, or longer temporal structure. It can still learn useful synthetic mappings, but strong synthetic scores do not imply that those mappings are invariant to real timbre/capture changes.

The P1 localization is compatible with this concern:
- candidate true-state probability mean **0.0305**;
- silence probability mean **0.7171**;
- pitch-only top-1 **31.25%**;
- median absolute semitone error **11.5**;
- candidate P1 onset threshold crossing within +/-4 frames **18.75%**.

These diagnostics show poor transfer on the exposed P1 examples, but they do not identify the encoder architecture itself as the sole cause.

### 4. The frozen frontend may be part of the domain boundary, but this remains unresolved

The preprocessing contract applies:
- RMS normalization;
- 192-bin VQT/CQT-like representation;
- per-capture maximum-referenced dB scaling;
- `/80 + 1` feature scaling;
- hop 512 at 22,050 Hz.

The prepared-feature localization found lower novelty on P2 than synthetic and P1 examples, but those values are feature-domain measurements. They cannot separate source attack strength, capture gain/articulation, preprocessing behavior, or annotation/crop alignment.

The already-prepared zero-model integrity audit is specifically designed to resolve this boundary by measuring native audio, frozen resampled audio, RMS-normalized audio and prepared CQT without changing the model.

## Strategy decision

### NO-GO: further synthetic model tuning from the S12/S13 results

Do not create S14, do not tune transform strength/fraction, do not change sampler/loss/threshold/decoder, and do not try another architecture merely because S13 failed.

The project currently lacks enough causal evidence to know whether another synthetic model experiment would attack the real bottleneck. Another search would risk optimizing against the simulator rather than improving real-domain transfer.

### GO: model-free preparation-integrity audit is the next informative experiment, but it remains behind the explicit P1/P2 source-access boundary

The existing package is already frozen and synthetically verified:
- `docs/astra/P2_ATTACK_PREPARATION_INTEGRITY_AUDIT_SPEC_V1.json`;
- `astra_backend/evaluation/p2_attack_preparation_integrity_audit_v1.py`;
- authorization request `docs/astra/P2_ATTACK_PREPARATION_INTEGRITY_AUDIT_AUTHORIZATION_REQUEST_V1.json`.

It loads **no model**, performs **no inference**, runs **zero optimizer steps**, changes no threshold, keeps P3 sealed, and is bounded to the exact eight previously identified P1/P2 captures.

This review does **not** execute that audit and does not reinterpret the user's generic continuation instruction as fresh real-media authorization.

## Decision tree after an authorized integrity audit

1. **Reproducible coordinate or preprocessing defect**
   - freeze the defect;
   - reproduce it with synthetic/unit fixtures;
   - implement one versioned frontend/preparation correction offline;
   - do not retrain a model until the correction has independent regression coverage;
   - any P1/P2 re-evaluation remains separately bounded.

2. **Raw P2 attacks are genuinely weaker while preparation is faithful**
   - treat this as capture/performance-domain evidence;
   - stop feature-space attack-warp searches;
   - if future synthetic work is justified, design a source-audio simulator/augmentation whose distributions are prospectively defined from broader development evidence, not tuned to the eight exposed examples.

3. **Raw attacks are strong but prepared CQT novelty is selectively reduced**
   - treat the frontend as a representation candidate;
   - first design a model-free frontend comparison with fixed invariants;
   - do not combine a new frontend and new model architecture in one experiment.

4. **Systematic annotation/crop offset**
   - correct the coordinate transform and add regression tests;
   - preserve historical results;
   - do not compensate by widening decoder windows or lowering thresholds.

5. **Mixed/unresolved evidence**
   - freeze unresolved;
   - do not continue synthetic architecture/augmentation search;
   - require broader development data or a new measurement plan before further model work.

## What is explicitly not justified yet
